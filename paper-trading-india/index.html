Paper trading lets you test intraday option ideas with virtual money. It only helps if it is honest about prices and costs. Bhagad Billa's paper trading is built to be strict.

What makes paper trading honest

  • Buy at the ask, sell at the bid. You pay the spread both ways, as you would in real trading.
  • Real contracts. Trades use the real option chain, expiry and strike.
  • Costs counted. Brokerage, STT, exchange charges, GST and stamp duty are included.
  • Time exits. Intraday trades are closed by the end of the session.

How to read your results

One good day proves nothing, because trades on the same day usually move with the same market. Look at the number of days and trades, the average result after costs and the worst losing streak. A loss limit protects you while you learn.

A simple practice plan

  1. Paper-trade for at least a few weeks and record every trade.
  2. Set a daily loss limit and stop when you reach it.
  3. Review which setups lost most often and avoid them.
  4. Move to real money only with an amount you can afford to lose, and start small.

Frequently asked questions

What is paper trading?

Paper trading means placing trades with virtual money at real market prices to test an idea without risking capital.

Is paper trading realistic?

It can be, if it fills at the ask, exits at the bid and charges brokerage, taxes and other costs. Bhagad Billa's paper trades do this.

How long should I paper trade before using real money?

Until you have enough closed trades to see a pattern. We use at least 60 closed trades before treating a signal as proven, and results from one strong day mean very little.
