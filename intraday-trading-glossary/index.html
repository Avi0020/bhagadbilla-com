Intraday and options trading glossary

Plain-language meanings of CE, PE, ATM, ITM, delta, theta, bid-ask spread, stop-loss, trigger and lot size.

Plain-language meanings of the terms you will see on an intraday option call.

CE (call option)
A contract that gains value when the underlying price rises.
PE (put option)
A contract that gains value when the underlying price falls.
Strike price
The price at which the option can be exercised.
ATM, ITM and OTM
At the money means the strike is close to the current price. In the money options already have value if exercised; out of the money options do not.
Premium
The price you pay to buy an option.
Lot size
The fixed number of units in one contract. Your profit or loss per option point is multiplied by the lot size.
Bid, ask and spread
The bid is the highest price a buyer offers, the ask is the lowest price a seller wants, and the spread is the gap between them.
Delta
How much the option price moves for a one-point move in the underlying.
Theta
How much value an option loses each day from the passage of time.
Implied volatility (IV)
The market's expected size of price moves. High IV makes options more expensive.
Open interest (OI)
The number of contracts that are still open. Higher OI usually means easier trading.
Trigger
The price level at which a trade setup is confirmed.
Stop-loss
A pre-decided exit price that limits the loss on a trade.
Target
A pre-decided exit price to book profit.
Risk-to-reward ratio
The potential profit compared with the potential loss, for example 1:2.
Expiry
The date on which the contract ends. Nifty and Bank Nifty have weekly and monthly expiries.
ATR
Average true range, a measure of how far a price normally moves in a period.
Paper trading
Practising trades with virtual money at real prices.
