Nifty and Bank Nifty option signals

What the scanner looks at on Nifty 50, Bank Nifty and F&O stocks, and which expiry and strike a call uses.

Register for free

Nifty and Bank Nifty option signals are only useful if the contract behind them can actually be traded. This page explains what the Bhagad Billa scanner looks at and how it picks a contract.

What the scanner reads

  • Price trend. 5-minute and 15-minute candles, moving averages and momentum.
  • Volume and volatility. Whether the move has real participation, and how large a normal move is, measured with ATR.
  • Option chain. Open interest, put-call ratio and the live bid and ask for each strike.
  • Time of day. Signals in the first and last minutes of the session are treated with extra care.

How a contract is chosen

StepRule
DirectionCall (CE) for an upward setup, put (PE) for a downward one.
ExpiryNot the contract that is about to expire; the scanner rolls to the next expiry when under two days remain.
StrikeAt or near the money, ranked by expected cost including time decay and spread.
LiquidityNeeds trading volume, open interest and a reasonable spread, or it is blocked.

What to check before you act

Always confirm the expiry in the contract name matches the price you see, and check the bid and ask in your own broker app before placing an order. Prices move quickly and a screen can lag.

Frequently asked questions

Which instruments does the scanner cover?

Nifty 50, Bank Nifty and large F&O stocks such as Reliance, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Infosys, TCS, SBI, ITC, L&T and Axis Bank.

Which expiry does a call use?

A call shows the exact expiry. When the nearest expiry is about to end, time decay is much faster, so the scanner prefers the next expiry for intraday trades.

Why are some contracts blocked?

Contracts with no recent trading, very low open interest or a very wide bid-ask spread are blocked, because you could not trade them at a fair price.
